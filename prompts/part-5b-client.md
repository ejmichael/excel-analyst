## The design (decided; follow it)
**Idea:** a report that reads like a good one-page statement from a small business: a heavy brand rule, a big title, a plain sentence per answer, and quiet statement-style tables. The brand header is the one memorable thing; everything else is restrained.

**Paper and colour (tokens):** paper `#FFFFFF`; ink `#16231F`; muted text `#56625C`; hairline `#D9D8CE`; brand = the user's `brand.color` or the app accent `#1E7A5F`; table header tint = brand mixed 9% into white (`color-mix(in srgb, var(--brand) 9%, #fff)`). Chart series: brand first, then `#E0A100`, `#1B6FA8`, `#C77DA5`, `#4FB0E8`. Use `print-color-adjust: exact` for every filled thing.
**Contrast guard (new, tested):** the brand colour is used for the top rule, the chart's first series, table-header tint and the tick mark. If the brand colour has less than 3:1 contrast against white, use a darkened version of it (same hue, step the lightness down until it passes) for the chart series and the tick mark. Never put text on the brand colour in print.
**Type (existing fonts, no new ones):** Space Grotesk 600 for the title (29pt, tight leading) and answer headings (14pt); Inter for everything else. Body narrative 10.5pt/1.55 with a maximum line length of about 37em; table text 8.5pt, header 8pt semibold, all numbers right-aligned with tabular figures; question line 8.5pt muted. Sentence case everywhere; no all-caps labels, no eyebrows, no numbering.
**Layout (A4 portrait, margins 18mm 16mm 20mm, one column):**
1. **Masthead on page 1:** a 3.2mm full-width brand-colour rule; below it an identity line with the logo (if `brand.logo_url`; max 12mm tall, `object-fit: contain`; no logo means no mark, never invent initials) and the business name (nothing if null) on the left and the date on the right; then the report title (Space Grotesk, max about 20 characters per line, wraps), then the subtitle and period if present (13pt muted). Notes, if present, sit under the title as plain text. No filled band, no card, no border box.
2. **Answers flow down the page with no cards.** Each answer: heading, the question in quotes (muted, small), the narrative as the lead paragraph, then the chart or table, then one quiet line: a tick and "All checks passed". Separate answers with generous space and one hairline above each answer except the first on the report. **Remove the per-answer timestamp** in print and on the public page.
3. **Tables (statement style):** tint header row with a 0.75pt ink rule under it, hairlines between rows, no vertical lines and no outer border; header row repeats on every page; rows never split; wrapping allowed, never clipped. Numbers never break mid-figure.
4. **Charts:** full content width; value labels at the bar ends; category labels on horizontal bars get about 36% of the width and wrap to two lines (break long strings such as URLs anywhere) with an ellipsis only after two lines; stacked and multi-series charts get a legend below. "View as table" content stays hidden in print.
5. **Page breaks (rules, in order of importance):** heading, question and narrative of an answer always stay together with the first part of its content (`break-after: avoid` on them; the narrative itself never splits). A **chart answer is never split** (keep its heading, narrative, chart and check line together). A **table answer may break between rows** (at least 3 rows on each side, header repeated). The "All checks passed" line never sits alone at the top of a page (`break-before: avoid`). Do **not** force one answer per page and do not keep whole table answers together: that is what left pages half empty. Decide "chart or table" from the answer's chart type, and use the answer's row count only to apply a break rule; never compute or look at a figure.
6. **Flagged answers** keep their warning in words, in print and on the public page: a "Check the numbers" note under the narrative with a 1.2mm amber left rule, text `#7A4F00` on `#FFF4D6`, stating that a check did not pass; it replaces the "All checks passed" line. Never hide or soften it.
7. **Running footer on every page** (CSS `@page` margin boxes): left "<report title>, <business name>" (just the title if there is no business name), right "Page X of N", 8pt muted. **Closing note** after the last answer, kept together and small: a heavy ink rule, then "Check important figures against your source file." and "Printed <date>." and, when `watermark` is true, the "Made with <APP_NAME>" line. No page may contain only the closing note: if it would land alone, keep the last answer's final lines with it.
8. **"How this was calculated" is not printed** (a closed disclosure on paper is a dead bar). Instead: on the **signed-in owner's** print, add one sentence to the closing note: "The working for each figure is in the Excel download." On the public page and its print, add no such sentence. On screen, the disclosures stay exactly as they are (the public page still shows them only when the payload includes SQL or checks). Never print source rows.
9. **Browser header and footer:** set `document.title` to the report's title while the report page is mounted (restore the previous title on leave) so the browser's header and the saved file name say the report's title, not "Analyst". Try to keep the browser's own header and footer out of the print (for example by relying on the `@page` margin boxes); if the CSS cannot suppress them in Chrome, add one short hint under the Download PDF button: "In the print window, turn off Headers and footers." State which of the two you did.

**Public page on screen (`/r/:token`):** the same document look, shown as a white sheet (max width about 210mm, subtle shadow) centred on a warm grey `#E9E8E1` desk; on a 390px phone, full width with 16px gutters and no shadow. A slim bar above the sheet holds only "Download PDF" (hidden in print) and, if `watermark` is true, nothing else. Keep: no owner controls, no sign-in, no `Authorization` header, the unavailable page, noindex and no-referrer, the logo refetch-once, all text rendered as text. Tables scroll sideways inside their own box on phones; charts scale to the width.
